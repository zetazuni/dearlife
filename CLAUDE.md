# Dearlife: start here

A cozy 3D house game made by Amir Ariffin (Zetazuni) for Lily. It is a 3D remake of the 2D Tiramisu App, with a camera that spins a full 360 degrees around the house. Models are made in Blender, the game is built in Unity.

If you are a new conversation or a new account, read these in order before doing anything:

1. **`docs/RULES.md`**: Amir's rules. Always follow them. (Short version: keep these notes updated, public repo so no secrets, everything on S:\, human wording with no em dashes, cinematic movie-like lighting and reflections, and **PC first**: high poly, detailed PBR textures, realistic physics, HDRP. and rule 7: **play like The Sims 4** (controls, build and buy mode, money, needs and mood, pie menu interactions), original art only, see `docs/SIMS4_PLAN.md`.)
2. **`docs/GAME_DESIGN.md`**: what the game is, what comes over from the 2D version and the phase plan.
3. **`docs/PIPELINE.md`**: how Blender and Unity connect through MCP, units, folders, naming and the asset workflow.
4. **`docs/CHARACTER_PLAN.md`**: the plan for realistic people (rule 8) and the character creator. Read it before touching characters.
5. **`docs/devlog.md`**: what happened in each session and what is next. The last entry tells you where things stand.

## Architecture

One short paragraph per system. Keep these accurate as the code changes. All runtime code is in the `Dearlife` namespace.

- **World coordinates:** 1 tile = 1 m. X runs along the house (0 living room end, 30 garage end), Z goes from the back wall (0) to the front glass (8) and on into the garden (up to about 22), so the garden is toward +Z. Ground floor top is y 0, the upper floor top is y 3.3 (`UPY`), walls are 3 m high, the slab under the house is 0.3 m and the garden ground sits at y -0.3. This matches the 2D game's grid (2D x = X, 2D y = Z) so furniture positions can be copied across.
- **Render setup:** HDRP 17.6 (`Assets/Settings/Dearlife_HDRP.asset`, global settings in `Assets/HDRPDefaultResources`), DirectX 12 only, linear colour, legacy Input Manager (no Input System package; mouse, touch and keys all go through `UnityEngine.Input`). URP was removed in session 4.
- **House builder** (`Assets/Editor/GreyboxBuilder.cs`): menu **Dearlife > Build greybox house** rebuilds `Main.unity` from scratch (house, garden, sun, volume, camera, furniture, physics). **Dearlife > Set up render pipeline** makes and assigns the HDRP asset and the physics project settings. Change the numbers in the builder and rerun it, do not hand-edit the scene. `Wall()` builds a wall line with door gaps (and lintels above 2.3 m), `Room()` makes a floor plate, a `RoomMarker`, a light and a reflection probe. Architecture materials (`Assets/Art/Materials/Architecture`) come from `MakeMaterials()`.
- **Camera** (`Scripts/CameraRig/OrbitCamera.cs`): orbit around `pivot` with `yaw` (free 360), `pitch` (12 to 85) and `distance` (3 to 70). Input writes to private targets (`tPivot/tYaw/tPitch/tDist`) and the real values ease toward them every frame. Left drag spins (after a 6 px threshold, otherwise it counts as a click in `ClickedThisFrame` for picking furniture later), right or middle drag pans, wheel zooms; touch has one finger spin, two finger pinch, pan and twist. `FocusOn`, `FitHouse`, `SetPivotHeight` are what other scripts call. `IsOverUi` is set by the HUD so drags on buttons do not move the camera.
- **Floors and walls** (`Scripts/House/HouseView.cs`, `WallCutaway.cs`): `HouseView` has the view (Ground, Upper with the ground still visible below, Whole house with the roof) and the wall mode (Auto, Up, Down). Keys 1/2/3 switch floors, Tab cycles wall mode, F fits the house. Every straight wall line is a `WallCutaway` with its pieces as children; in Auto mode a wall cuts down to a 0.25 m stub when the camera and the focus point are on opposite sides of it. The focus is the camera pivot clamped inside the house footprint (`houseMin/houseMax`) so the front glass still cuts when you look from the garden. Whole house view keeps every wall up.
- **Rooms** (`Scripts/House/RoomMarker.cs`): one per room with display name, floor, size and order; the HUD lists the rooms of the active floor and the camera jumps to them.
- **Textures and materials** (`Assets/Editor/MaterialLibrary.cs`, `tools/fetch_textures.py`): 20 CC0 Poly Haven sets at 2K in `Assets/Art/Textures/<id>/` (colour jpg, OpenGL normal jpg, HDRP mask png packed as R metallic, G AO, B detail, A smoothness, plus a height png kept for later). `textures.json` holds each set's real size so it tiles at true scale. `Textured()` makes an HDRP/Lit material with Planar (floors), Triplanar (walls and blocks, so scaled cubes never stretch) or UV0 (Blender models, UVs in metres) mapping and a smoothness range; `Plain()`, `Glass()` (thin refraction, SSR on transparent) and `Water()` too. Every material goes through `HDMaterial.ValidateMaterial`. Run `python tools/fetch_textures.py` to add sets.
- **Film look** (`Assets/Editor/CinematicSetup.cs`, rules 5 and 6): `Pipeline()` turns on SSR (also on transparents), SSGI, SSAO, volumetrics and volumetric clouds, water, decals, ray tracing support, 4096 PCSS soft directional shadows, contact and screen space shadows, forces DX12. `PostVolume()` rebuilds `Assets/Settings/Cinematic.asset`: physically based sky with a cloud layer, volumetric fog, automatic histogram exposure (EV 7 to 15, +0.4), 4 cascade shadows to 90 m, contact shadows, SSR, SSGI, AO, ACES, bloom, grade, white balance, cool shadows and warm highlights, vignette, film grain, physical camera depth of field and light motion blur. Lights use physical units: the sun is 100000 lux at 6000 K, each room has a 900 lumen point light (2700 K, 4000 K in the garage and gym) and a box shaped baked HD reflection probe. Camera: physical 28 mm on a 36 x 24 sensor, f/2.8, TAA high. `Bake()` bakes the probes (no lightmaps). `Scripts/Graphics/CinematicFocus.cs` keeps focus at the orbit distance.
- **Physics** (`Assets/Editor/PhysicsSetup.cs`, `Scripts/Physics/PhysicsPoke.cs`): 90 Hz fixed step, 12/4 solver iterations. Physics materials in `Assets/Art/Physics` (Wood, Stone, Glass, Grass, Rubber, Metal, Fabric, Foliage) are matched to colliders by render material name (`AssignSurfaces`). Furniture: every part mesh gets a MeshCollider (convex when the piece can move) and the root gets a Rigidbody with a real mass from `Specs` (sofa 70 kg, table 38 kg, cushion 0.8 kg, rug static). Cushions are dropped from 0.9 m and settle on the sofa by themselves. `PhysicsPoke` shoves whatever movable thing you click, scaled by its mass (a test tool until decorate mode).
- **Furniture** (`Assets/Editor/FurnitureImport.cs` + the builder's `Layout` table): see `docs/PIPELINE.md`. Blender slot names map to textured materials in `FurnitureImport.Looks`. Pieces so far (high poly, one child per part): `sofa`, `marbletable`, `geomrug` (with tassels), `cushion` x2 in the living room, and the kitchen set (session 6, built by `tools/blender_kitchen.py` into `Blender/furniture_kitchen.blend`): `kitchenrun` (counter with sink, cooktop, hood, wall units and oven tower), `fridge`, `kitchenisland` (waterfall marble, walnut slats), `barstool` x3, `diningtable`, `diningchair` x4 and `pendant` x3 (glowing bulb plus a warm point light added by the builder). Bathroom and garage (`tools/blender_bathgarage.py` into `Blender/furniture_bathgarage.blend`, reuses the toolkit at the top of `blender_kitchen.py`): `shower`, `vanity`, `bathmirror` (attached to the back wall so it hides when the wall drops), `toilet`, `bathtub`, `towelrack`, `bathmat`, `washer`, `dryer`, `basket`, then `sedan` and `mpv` (lofted cars with wheel arches, glass and lights), `workbench`, `garageshelf`, `toolchest`, `bicycle`, `evcharger`. Small props (`tools/blender_props.py` into `Blender/props_home.blend`: `fruitbowl`, `cuttingboard`, `mug`, `espresso`, `utensils`, `herbs`, `towelstack`, `candles`, `cardboardboxes`, `paintcans`, `sparetyres`) are placed on surfaces from the builder's `Tabletop` table (id, x, surface height y, z, rotation) and are all real rigid bodies. Upper floor (`tools/blender_upper1.py` and `blender_upper2.py` into `Blender/furniture_upper1.blend` and `furniture_upper2.blend`): Teacher's Room (`platformbed`, `nightstand`, `bedlamp`, `wardrobe`, `bookcase` with hundreds of books, `teacherdesk`, `officechair`, `globe`, `chalkboard`, `worldmap`), Office & Library (`officedesk` with monitor, `filecabinet`, `uplight` with a warm light, `whiteboard`), Engineer's Room (`platformbed_e`, `ebench`, `robotarm`, `printer3d`, `beanbag`) and Gym (`treadmill`, `dumbbells`, `weightbench` with barbell, `spinbike`, `punchbag`, `yogamat`, `waterdispenser`, `gymmirror`). Upper floor items use `floor` 1 in `Layout`; `Tabletop` entries with y above 2 m go on the upper floor. `UpperProps` holds the Poly Haven chairs and plants up there. Wall pieces (chalkboard, worldmap, whiteboard, gym mirrors) are added to their wall's `WallCutaway.attachments` through `AttachTo`. Car windows are real edge loops in the cabin loft (glass and paint are picked per face, not by position). The shower is a fully glazed cubicle (front with a door, side and ceiling glass, no beam). Cars have glowing tail lights plus a red point light each. Cars and fixed fittings are static, small things (basket, tool chest, stools, chairs) are rigid bodies. New material names: `Cabinet_main`, `Stainless`, `Brass`, `GlassDark`, `Bulb`, `Ceramic`, `ClearGlass`, `CarGlass` (both use the glass material), `Mirror`, `Rattan`, `PaintRed`, `Car_main`, `CarSilver`, `Tire`, `Headlight`, `Taillight` (bulbs and car lights glow).
- **Photoscanned props** (`Assets/Editor/PropPlacer.cs`, `tools/fetch_models.py`): Poly Haven glTF models imported by glTFast. The builder's `LivingProps` and `GardenProps` tables place them (variant, position, rotation, scale, physics body, mass). See `docs/PIPELINE.md`.
- **Architecture details:** glass walls get black steel mullions (at most 1.5 m apart) and rails via `Frames()` in the builder, as children of the wall line so they cut away with it. The roof is flat with a deep front overhang, an oak soffit and a black steel fascia. `WallCutaway.attachments` hides things hung on a wall (the picture above the sofa) while that wall is cut down.
- **Graphics modes** (`Scripts/Graphics/GraphicsModes.cs`): Ultra (DLSS Quality, ray traced reflections, full resolution SSGI on high), Quality (default: DLSS Quality, screen space reflections, half resolution SSGI on low), Performance (DLSS Balanced, no SSGI, no volumetric fog, no contact shadows, 2 cascades). G key or the HUD button cycles, saved in PlayerPrefs. Falls back to native TAA without DLSS. DLSS needs `com.unity.modules.nvidia` plus dynamic resolution and DLSS switched on in the HDRP asset (`CinematicSetup.Pipeline`). Static foliage is excluded from ray tracing to keep Ultra affordable.
- **Benchmark** (`Scripts/Graphics/FpsBenchmark.cs`): F9 measures every mode and Quality with each effect off (2 s warm up, 6 s measure) and logs to the Console and `benchmark.txt` in `%USERPROFILE%/AppData/LocalLow/DefaultCompany/Dearlife by Zetazuni/`. Claude sets the Game view to 1920x1080 with `PlayModeWindow.SetCustomRenderingResolution` before measuring.
- **Version** (`Scripts/GameInfo.cs`): `Version`, `BuildDate`, `RepoUrl`, shown bottom right by the HUD.
- **Title screen** (`Scripts/UI/MainMenu.cs`): `MainMenu.Active` (true at boot, set again by `MainMenu.Open()`) gates the whole HUD (`HouseHud`, `SimUi`, `Tutorial`, and the P/V/M key toggles for decorate, build and the map all check it) so nothing else can show while it's up. A fixed indigo panel (not tied to `Ui.Dark`, like `Splash`), 30% of the width including its fade, with a big script-font title (its glow is centred on `Ui.TextWidth("Dearlife", ...)`, not a guessed rect), the splash's gradient rule, sixteen slow floating sparkles and a row of feature chips - fades into the live house on the rest of the screen through `FadeTex()` (a bilinear-filtered gradient texture, one draw call; the first version was 40 flat rects and visibly banded); the camera is `OrbitCamera` with `ExternalControl = true`, driven each frame by `SetImmediate` through a loop of shots built from `RoomMarker.All` (no hand-tuned coordinates) with a slow yaw drift and zoom while holding, and a `SmoothStep` blend moving to the next. It is its own small page-based UI (`enum Page { Title, NewSlots, LoadSlots, Settings }`), **not** the in-game `SettingsWindow` - opening that one from here used to draw behind the fade (no explicit `GUI.depth`, lost to `MainMenu`'s -500), so instead the Settings page is drawn by `MainMenu` itself, reusing `SettingsWindow.DrawGraphics`/`Drop` (`public static`, one implementation) for Window mode/Resolution/V-Sync/Frame limit/Quality, plus Look and just the master volume - none of the in-game-only tabs (Time, hints, layout reset, Exit, which lives as its own button on the Title page instead). Escape closes one layer at a time (`SettingsWindow.DropdownOpen`/`CloseDropdowns`, then a confirm dialog, then back to the Title page); a `←` button does the same for the mouse. **Five save slots** (`SaveSystem.MaxSlots`), one column, tall cards: Start and Load Game open a picker (`SaveSystem.SlotSummary`/`SlotLastSaved` for each tile); choosing one calls `SaveSystem.SetActiveSlot` (+ `NewGame(slot)` to wipe), sets a static `pendingEnter` flag and reloads the scene (`SceneManager.LoadScene`, registered in Build Settings) so every system's `Awake()` reads the new slot fresh; `MainMenu.Awake()` sees `pendingEnter` and skips straight to gameplay instead of showing the menu again.
- **Save slots** (`Scripts/Sim/SaveSystem.cs`): every system that saves reads and writes its key through `SaveSystem.Key(base)`, which suffixes it with `ActiveSlot` (slot 1 keeps the old unsuffixed keys, so pre-slot saves still load). The state-key list (funds, built, painted, bought, sold, layout, windows, dogs, hour, season, tutorial, the two sims' careers) is what `NewGame(slot)` wipes - settings (audio, dark mode, graphics, display) are deliberately left out, so they're shared across every slot. **Gotcha:** `PurchaseSave` in `BuyMode.cs` is a plain static class that caches its loaded data in a field; a scene reload does *not* clear static fields (only a domain reload does), so `SetActiveSlot` explicitly calls `PurchaseSave.ResetCache()`. Any future static cache like that needs the same treatment. `SaveNow()` (wired to Settings' "Save now" and to Exit), `HasSave`, `LastSavedText`.
- **UI** (`Scripts/UI/Ui.cs` and the panels): the whole interface copies the 2D Tiramisu App (cream `#fff8ef` cards, pink `#f4a6b7` borders, round pills, Nunito for words, Great Vibes for the title, fonts in `Assets/Resources/Fonts`, static Nunito weights cut from the variable font with fontTools). `Ui` is a small IMGUI toolkit: `Round`, `Ring`, `Box`, `Pill`, `Chip`, `Square`, `CardButton`, `Bar`, `Slider`, `Scroll`, `Label`, `Tag`, `Bubble`. All rectangles are in "ui units" (900 units high) and get multiplied by `Ui.Scale` at draw time (the GUI matrix is NOT scaled, so text stays sharp). Rounded shapes come from `GUI.DrawTexture` with border radii, no texture files. `HouseHud` = top bar (title, money, date, speed, Undo and Redo while decorating or building, Move, Settings), camera buttons on the left, hint, the 350 wide side panel with tabs Home, Family, Shop, Build, and the vertical Hide panel tab on the edge of the panel (`DrawHandle`, key P too). `SettingsWindow` (top bar button) has tabs Sound, Time, Graphics and Game (light and dark mode, hints). The Graphics tab (`Scripts/Graphics/DisplaySettings.cs`) has dropdown pickers (`SettingsWindow.Drop`) for window mode (Windowed, Borderless windowed, Fullscreen), resolution (greyed out in borderless), V-Sync and a frame limit (30/60/120/144/Unlimited, greyed out with V-Sync on), saved per PC and applied on the standalone exe's startup only (`RuntimeInitializeOnLoadMethod`, skipped in the Editor so the Game view is untouched); the Ultra/Quality/Performance picture-quality buttons (`GraphicsModes`) live in the same tab. `Scripts/UI/LoadingScreen.cs` is a Sims-style loading screen (place name in script, a filling rose/violet/cyan bar, a rotating tip): shown at startup until `DearlifeNav.Ready` and `GameAudio.MusicReady` are both true, and on every `LotManager.Travel` trip in place of the old plain fade. **Dark mode** (`Ui.Dark`, saved in `dearlife.dark`) is the 2D game's neon night look: `Ui.Ink/Soft/Card/Line/Pink/Accent/...` are properties that switch palette, `Ui.Surface`, `Ui.Panel`, `Ui.OnAccent` are the fills and text that flip too, and `Ui.OnFill` draws the pink to ice blue gradient for switched-on things (`HouseHud.Show(tab)`; the Shop and Build tabs own `BuyMode.Active` and `BuildMode.Active`, drawn by `BuyMode.DrawShop` and `BuildMode.DrawBuild`). `SimUi` = needs card, status window, toasts. `LiveMode` = person cards and the round menu. `Splash` = the intro, `Tutorial` = the hint card. `OrbitCamera.IsOverUi` is chained by HouseHud, SimUi and LiveMode. Shop pictures are `Assets/Resources/Thumbs/<id>.png`, rendered by `tools/blender_thumbs.py` through the Blender MCP (FBX import, clay look).
- **Sound** (`Sim/GameAudio.cs`): ported from the 2D game: seasonal lofi music (same chord progressions, BPM and hooks, rendered offline in a background task into a looping `AudioClip` per season), its jingles (pop, coin, buy, error, love, level up), recorded `cat_meow` and `cat_purr` (also `dog_bark`, `dog_panting`, `guitar`, `synth`, waiting for a dog and instruments) in `Assets/Resources/Audio`. New in 3D: sizzle for cooking, running water for washing and swimming, birds, crickets, wind. Volume sliders (everything, music, sounds, pets) are in the Settings tab and saved.
- **Decorate mode** (`Scripts/House/DecorateMode.cs`, `Furniture.cs`): key M or the HUD button. Every piece the builder places gets a `Furniture` component with a stable key ("sofa#0") and a `pinned` flag (built in fittings and wall pieces, see `Pinned` in the builder). Press on a piece to pick it up: it is made kinematic and follows whatever surface is under the mouse (raycast, walls and roof ignored), snapped to 5 cm (Ctrl = free), R turns it. Pieces standing on it (origin above its base and inside its footprint) ride along. `IsFree` checks an oriented box against everything except the surface it stands on and its riders, and a piece dropped in a wall goes back to the last free spot. The layout is saved to PlayerPrefs (`dearlife.layout`, JSON, entries by key so new furniture does not break old saves) after every drop and loaded in `Start`. `OrbitCamera.Blocked` stops the camera from spinning while a piece is held, and `PhysicsPoke` is off in decorate mode.
- **Pool ripples** (`Scripts/Water/PoolRipples.cs`): the pool surface is a 98 x 49 grid mesh driven by a 60 Hz wave equation (reflecting walls, damping 0.985), with normals from the slope so screen space reflections show the rings. Falling things splash by impact speed, moving ones leave a wake, a click on the water drops a stone, and a breeze adds drips. Every rigid body in the pool gets Archimedes buoyancy from the bounding box of all its parts (`fillFactor` 0.4 of the box is solid) plus water drag, so cushions and tables float and heavy things sink slowly.
- **Day and night** (`Scripts/Graphics/DayNightCycle.cs`, `SwitchableLight.cs`): hour 0 to 24 (saved), sun elevation 68 degrees at noon, moon opposite it, a generated starfield cubemap as the sky's space emission, exposure limits open up at night (EV 4.2 minimum), reflection probes fall to 0.02 percent at night (they were baked in full daylight, at 10 percent every rough surface still glowed like it was sunny). Room lights are big soft rectangle panels in the ceiling (`RoomLightAndProbe`), pendants, downlights, lamps and pool lamps are small area lights too (`AddNightLight` with an area size), so nothing makes a hot spot. Bloom and exposure compensation drop a little at night. Lights that should change carry a `SwitchableLight` with a day and a night lumen value (room lights 500 to 1100, pendants, uplight, bedside lamps, path bollards, roof downlights, pool lamps). The time panel is top right in the HUD (slider, four presets, "Let time run" = 20 seconds per hour). Only one directional light casts shadows at a time, the sun wins.
- **Model orientation gotcha:** a Blender piece faces -Y, which arrives facing +Z, so it is turned half a turn. Blender +X therefore arrives as Unity -X (left and right swap when you place things by world x). The shower was wrong because of this. Check side-specific parts in Unity coordinates.
- **Modelling gotchas found by looking:** never put a door or panel with its front face exactly level with the carcass front, the two faces fight and flicker in diagonal stripes (the kitchen wall units did this, doors now stand 1 cm in front). Directional lights have `normalBias` 1.6 and `slopeBias` 1.0 to keep shadow acne away from thin panels. The money trees stand on the `planter` model (soil at 0.38 m), moving a planter in decorate mode carries its tree.
- **Sliding doors** (`Scripts/House/SlidingDoor.cs`, `DoorOpener.cs`, builder `SlidingDoorAt` and `BuildDoors`): every doorway between rooms (four on the ground floor, four upstairs) has a modern door hung on a black rail in front of the wall: framed glass in glass walls, walnut with fine grooves in concrete walls. `slide` says how far and which way the panel runs. A door opens when the mouse hovers over it (one raycast per frame for all doors), or when anything with a `DoorOpener` component is inside its sensor box (checked every 0.15 s), and shuts 1.2 s after the last one leaves. **People and pets: add `DoorOpener` to them and the doors open for them.** Doors are in their wall's `attachments`, so they hide with the wall when it is cut down.
- **Movable windows** (`Scripts/House/WindowWall.cs`, `WallWindowPart.cs`): the four concrete outer wall lines (back and left, both floors) are `WindowWall`s that build their own pieces around the door gaps and a list of windows (glass, slim black frame, oak sill) and rebuild when one moves. In decorate mode press on a window and drag it along its wall (10 cm grid, it cannot overlap another window, a door gap or the wall end), R changes the width (0.8, 1.2, 1.6, 2.2 m), Esc puts it back. Positions are saved in `dearlife.windows`. `WallCutaway.Refresh()` re-collects the pieces after a rebuild.

- **Realistic character materials** (`Editor/CharacterLook.cs`, `People/Eyes.cs`): only HDRP's built in shader graphs and textures made here (free only). `FurnitureImport.ImportCharacters` calls `CharacterLook.Upgrade` for models named `mpfb...`: the body gets `Hidden/HDRP/Skin` (subsurface scattering with HDRP's Skin diffusion profile, and `Assets/Art/Textures/Characters/skin_pores_detail.png`, a seamless pore detail map generated by code in HDRP's detail layout), `cut_` materials get `HDRP/Hair` with alpha clipping (head hair uses a `_strands` copy of its texture with the painted shine divided out by a heavy blur, tinted back to its own colour, so the creator can recolour it; keep the highlights low, MakeHuman card UVs make strong ones band across the crown), teeth a glossier Lit. `CharacterLook.MakeEye` makes `<model>_eye.mat` on `HDRP/Eye` with iris and sclera textures cut from MakeHuman's eye texture, and makes sure the Skin, Iris and Sclera profiles are in the default volume's Diffusion Profile List (HDRP renders unlisted profiles wrong). `Eyes` hides MakeHuman's eye mesh and hangs two eyeballs on the `head` bone, built in the geometry HDRP's eye shader assumes (EyeUtils.hlsl: cornea along +Z, iris radius 0.22 on the plane z = -0.02, our ball radius 0.44), sized from the width of MakeHuman's eye and placed by its front (their eye is only the front of a ball). `BodyShape.Apply` calls `Eyes.Refit`. `tools/blender_mpfb_body.py` paints a hair coloured scalp into our copy of the skin texture under the hair (`paint_scalp`), so partings do not show bare skin.
- **Motion capture** (`Editor/CharacterAnimation.cs`, **Dearlife > Set up character animation**, `tools/blender_cmu_clips.py`): 17 clips from the CMU library in `Assets/Art/Animations/CMU` (credits there), re-saved at 30 fps in Blender because the downloaded FBX files say 0 fps and Unity's Humanoid import then resamples them at 1 fps (three poses a clip). The menu makes the clips and `mpfb` models Humanoid with explicit bone maps (CMU uses Daz style names, MPFB Unreal style) and a T pose reference computed from the body's own axes (the CMU root is turned 270 degrees about X, so its "forward" is up; getting the arm roll wrong swings every elbow bend up), trims each recording to its best stretch (`Segments`, found by charting hip height and joint movement), and builds `Assets/Resources/Animation/People.controller`: one state per `CharacterRig.Pose` (`PoseClips`), any-state cross fades on the `pose` int, `walkSpeed` for the stride, IK pass on. `CharacterRig` with `kind = "mpfb"` and a Humanoid avatar drives that Animator instead of code posing (a walk order at no speed plays Stand). On seats it fits the recording to our furniture: in `OnAnimatorIK` the body is moved by an offset learnt frame by frame so the pelvis lands on the `UseSpot`, and each foot is planted under its knee on the floor or the stool's foot ring; `LateUpdate` snaps the rest and lifts torso and thighs by the spot's `raise` and `legRaise` for loungers and hammocks. Measured: 0.0 cm hip error and feet on the floor on all ten seat types.
